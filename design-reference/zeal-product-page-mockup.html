Colombia Coffee

A seven-day immersion across Colombia's coffee world — from Bogotá's award-winning cafés to remote Andean farms — learning to taste, process, and evaluate specialty coffee alongside champion baristas, Q-grade labs, and multi-generational producers.

Immersive Hands-on / active Expert-led Small-group Privileged access At-source / origin Skill-building

  • A "coffee baptism" and Q-grader-style tasting with a champion barista in Bogotá
  • Hands-on lab access at La Palma y El Tucán — sensory work usually reserved for certification students
  • Hand-harvesting at a remote Andean farm open only by private request
  • A green-coffee blending and cupping session at an export farm shipping to Dubai and beyond
  • Meeting the fifth-generation IWCA women producers at Primitivo

Adventure FAQs

Do I need any experience?

No. It starts from a sensory "baptism" and builds up — made for keen drinkers who want real depth, not trained baristas only.

What makes this different from a normal tour?

You get inside places enthusiasts can't book — a Q-grade lab, a private-access farm, an export blending session, IWCA women producers — and you do the work rather than watch.

How many people are in the group?

A small group of 6–10, plus two tour leaders.

What's included in the price?

Accommodation, breakfasts and listed meals, all farm visits and tastings, entrance fees, private transport for tours, English-speaking guides. Flights, arrival/departure transfers and tips are extra.

Who is this trip for?

Coffee-obsessed travellers who want to leave materially more expert — comfortable with early starts and one long travel day.

When does it run?

Low season 2027; specific dates to be confirmed.

How do I get there?

Fly into Bogotá (BOG); depart from Pereira. International and domestic flights aren't included.

Will I actually learn to cup and score coffee like a Q-grader?Trip-specific

Yes — you get direct, hands-on lab access at a farm that hosts the official Q Grader and Q Processing certifications, with sessions built around comparing fermentation processes and tasting flawed against excellent cups.

Do we visit real working farms, or just tourist-facing "coffee experiences"?Trip-specific

Both, deliberately contrasted — from an industry-training lab, to a farm that only receives visitors by private request, to a fifth-generation family operation, through to an industrial exporter processing beans for 60+ countries.

Is there a single supplement if I'm travelling solo?Trip-specific

Yes, there's a single-room surcharge — ask Zeal for the current rate.
